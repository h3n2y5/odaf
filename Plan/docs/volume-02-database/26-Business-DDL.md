# Chapter 26

# Business DDL

---

# 1. Purpose

This chapter defines the physical Oracle implementation standards for business repositories generated by the Oracle Dynamic Application Framework (ODAF).

Unlike the Core DDL, which defines the metadata platform itself, Business DDL specifies the physical database structures used by enterprise applications built on ODAF.

Business schemas SHALL be generated from metadata through the ODAF Compiler.

---

# 2. Design Objectives

Business DDL SHALL:

- generate business schemas from metadata;
- maintain consistency with Core DDL standards;
- preserve aggregate boundaries;
- support domain-driven design;
- support scalable enterprise deployments;
- remain compiler-generated.

---

# 3. Business Repository Architecture

```text
Business Metadata

↓

Compiler

↓

Business DDL

↓

Oracle Business Schema

↓

Runtime
```

Business schemas SHALL never be authored manually as the primary source.

---

# 4. Business Domains

Business repositories SHALL be organized by business domains.

| Prefix | Domain |
|----------|--------|
| MD_* | Master Data |
| PUR_* | Purchasing |
| INV_* | Inventory |
| SAL_* | Sales |
| FIN_* | Finance |
| HRM_* | Human Resources |
| CRM_* | Customer Relationship |
| PRJ_* | Project Management |
| MFG_* | Manufacturing |
| QMS_* | Quality Management |

Organizations MAY define additional business domains.

---

# 5. Aggregate Model

Each business domain SHALL be composed of Aggregate Roots.

Example:

```text
Purchasing

↓

Purchase Order

↓

Purchase Order Line

↓

Approval

↓

Receipt
```

Aggregate boundaries SHALL follow domain-driven design principles.

---

# 6. Standard Business Columns

Every Aggregate Root SHALL define the standard platform columns.

| Column | Type |
|----------|------|
| OBJECT_ID | RAW(16) |
| OBJECT_CODE | VARCHAR2(100) |
| OBJECT_NAME | VARCHAR2(200) |
| VERSION_NO | NUMBER |
| STATUS | VARCHAR2(30) |
| CREATED_AT | TIMESTAMP WITH TIME ZONE |
| UPDATED_AT | TIMESTAMP WITH TIME ZONE |
| CREATED_BY | RAW(16) |
| UPDATED_BY | RAW(16) |

Business-specific attributes SHALL be added after the standard column set.

---

# 7. Master Data Repository

Master Data repositories define reusable reference entities.

Typical examples include:

- Customer
- Supplier
- Warehouse
- Item
- Organization
- Currency
- Unit of Measure
- Tax Code

Master Data SHALL support versioning and lifecycle management.

---

# 8. Transaction Repository

Transactional repositories capture business events.

Examples include:

- Purchase Requisition
- Purchase Order
- Goods Receipt
- Sales Order
- Invoice
- Payment

Transactional objects SHALL reference Master Data using OBJECT_ID.

---

# 9. Financial Repository

Financial repositories include:

- Chart of Accounts
- Journal Entry
- Ledger
- Budget
- Cost Center
- Fiscal Calendar

Financial structures SHALL support auditing and period control.

---

# 10. Human Resources Repository

HR repositories MAY include:

- Employee
- Position
- Department
- Leave Request
- Attendance
- Payroll

Security policies SHALL protect sensitive business data.

---

# 11. Inventory Repository

Inventory repositories include:

- Item
- Stock Balance
- Warehouse
- Location
- Movement
- Reservation

Inventory transactions SHALL support traceability.

---

# 12. Customer Relationship Repository

CRM repositories include:

- Customer
- Contact
- Opportunity
- Campaign
- Service Request

CRM entities SHALL integrate with Notification and Workflow repositories.

---

# 13. Project Repository

Project repositories MAY include:

- Project
- Task
- Milestone
- Resource
- Timesheet

Projects SHALL support integration with financial and HR domains.

---

# 14. Constraints

Business repositories SHALL define:

- Primary Keys;
- Foreign Keys;
- Business Unique Constraints;
- Check Constraints;
- Domain-specific validation constraints.

Constraint names SHALL follow the Core DDL naming conventions.

---

# 15. Indexing

Indexes SHALL be generated automatically.

Recommended indexes include:

- business code;
- document number;
- status;
- transaction date;
- foreign keys.

Index generation SHALL be compiler-driven.

---

# 16. Relationships

Cross-domain relationships SHALL use immutable OBJECT_ID references.

Example:

```text
Purchase Order

↓

Supplier

↓

Currency

↓

Organization
```

Circular dependencies SHALL be avoided.

---

# 17. Runtime Mapping

Business metadata SHALL compile into runtime business repositories.

```text
Business Metadata

↓

Compiler

↓

Business Schema

↓

Runtime Package
```

Logical identity SHALL be preserved throughout compilation.

---

# 18. Migration Strategy

Business schema evolution SHALL be managed through metadata-driven migrations.

Supported operations include:

- create;
- alter;
- rename;
- archive;
- deprecate.

Migration plans SHALL be versioned.

---

# 19. Traceability

```text
Business Requirement

↓

Business Metadata

↓

Generated DDL

↓

Oracle Schema

↓

Runtime

↓

Audit
```

Every business object SHALL be traceable to its originating metadata.

---

# 20. Risks

Potential risks include:

- oversized aggregates;
- excessive cross-domain coupling;
- schema drift;
- inconsistent business naming;
- manual schema modification.

These risks SHALL be mitigated through compiler-generated DDL, governance validation, and domain ownership.

---

# 21. Summary

The Business DDL specification defines the Oracle implementation standards for enterprise business repositories generated by ODAF.

By applying consistent physical modeling rules, domain-driven aggregate design, compiler-generated DDL, and standardized platform columns, ODAF ensures that business schemas evolve with the same governance, versioning, and traceability guarantees as the metadata platform itself.
